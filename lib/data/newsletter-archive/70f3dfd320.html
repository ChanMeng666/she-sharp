Bridging Continents, Raising Questions

A word from Mahsa…

Kia ora  <<First Name>>

It's been a month that took me from a kitchen table conversation with my nine-year-old to a stage in Shenzhen, and both left me thinking about the same thing: how do we prepare people for a world that keeps moving faster than we can explain it?


I was glad to be part of the New Zealand Herald's piece on families raising kids in the age of AI. When my daughter Elena asked me why a video was fake, I turned the question back to her. I don't want her to be simply "the next AI user." I want her to carry judgement, ethics, and curiosity into whatever comes next.


From there, I moderated a panel at the APEC Tech for Good Workshop in Shenzhen on closing the gender gap in tech, sitting alongside changemakers from across Asia and the Pacific. One line from the APEC report has stayed with me since: technology should be judged not by efficiency, but by whether it lets those most at risk of being left behind meaningfully participate.


And now I can't wait for the Aotearoa AI Hackathon Festival on 7–8 August, She Sharp with AUT and the AI Forum at AUT City Campus.

Ngā mihi nui



Mahsa McCauley
Founder & Director, She Sharp New Zealand

Spotlight: AI Hackathon Festival 2026 🚀

On 7–8 August 2026, She Sharp is collaborating with AI Forum to host the AI Hackathon 2026 at AUT City Campus. This two-day, in-person event will bring together students, professionals, and AI enthusiasts to develop innovative AI-enabled solutions that address real-world challenges aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Participants will work in teams throughout the event. On Day 1, teams will be formed, problem statements will be defined, and participants will begin designing and developing their solutions. On Day 2, teams will refine their solutions and present their final pitches to a panel of judges.

Each venue will select one winning team to advance to the national judging stage. Four national finalist teams will then be invited to present their solutions at the Aotearoa AI Summit 2026.

No prior hackathon experience is required. Approximately one-third of participants are expected to be first-time hackathon attendees. Mentors from AUT and industry partners will be available throughout the event to provide guidance and support.

Recent Event Recap

Working Smarter: AI, MYOB & the New Delivery Landscape

On 30 July, She Sharp partnered with MYOB to host an evening exploring how AI is reshaping software delivery and the way people work, featuring three MYOB speakers followed by a hands-on group workshop.

Jean Fulop, Senior Product Manager at MYOB, opened with a look at the gap between AI that dazzles in a demo and AI you can trust with real work, sharing her guiding principle that every AI output is a draft, not a verdict, along with three mindset shifts: draft, not verdict; direct, don't do; and iterate, don't accept.

Marion Nicholas, Chartered Accountant and Domain Expert, walked through how AI supports her team's design and delivery workflow, from exploring layout options with Microsoft Copilot to preparing AI-ready handovers for developers. Her key lesson: faster coding doesn't make rework cheaper, so the real value comes from using AI earlier to challenge assumptions before building.

Katy Seel, GM People for SME & Practice, brought a people-first lens, framing MYOB's approach around mindset, skillset, and toolset, and sharing where individuals typically sit on their AI journey, from experimenting through to scaling. Her closing message stayed with the room: the toolset will keep changing, but mindset and skillset are what you own.

The evening closed with a collaborative AI problem-solving activity, where attendees chose their path by experience level and worked through a simple, reusable process: start, clarify, refine, review, improve. Attendees left with practical tools for using AI with more judgement, and a reminder that the first answer AI gives is rarely the final one worth accepting.
