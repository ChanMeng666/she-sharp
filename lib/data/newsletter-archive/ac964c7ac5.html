From Rio to UNESCO: Journey of Global Impact and Local Leadership

A word from Mahsa…

🌟 Kia ora  <<First Name>> 🌟






The past few months have been a vibrant journey across borders and ideas — from assessing transformative digital innovations in Rio de Janeiro with the World Summit Awards, to beginning a new chapter as Communication and Information Commissioner for the New Zealand National Commission for UNESCO.
These experiences highlight a shared mission across Aotearoa and the world: using technology and education to drive social good, protect human rights, and foster inclusive growth. From advancing ethical AI and digital literacy to empowering underrepresented voices, the momentum continues to grow.
To continue this kaupapa, I warmly invite you to join us for a special International Women’s Day event on 6 March 2026, where She Sharp and our partners will bring together diverse voices across technology, education, and community to celebrate, connect, and spark meaningful action for gender equity.
As 2026 unfolds, I’m excited to keep building bridges between global collaboration and local action — ensuring that innovation, equity, and opportunity remain at the heart of our collective progress.

AI Empowerment: Shaping an Inclusive Digital Future


On 21 November 2025, SheSharp and HCLTech hosted an inspiring evening exploring how AI can be a powerful catalyst for more inclusive innovation, careers, and workplaces. Attendees heard how AI is already helping people unlock new levels of creativity, productivity, and problem-solving, while also confronting the responsibility to design fair, ethical, and human-centred systems.


The night featured a keynote from Rasha Y. Abu‑Safieh, Head of Digital Strategy & Partnerships at UO, who shared insights on digital strategy, partnerships, and the role of diverse voices in shaping responsible AI. A panel of industry leaders – Graeme Riley (CIO, Dunedin City Council), Deb Sutton (Labour Market Advisor, MSD), Sophie Wiltshier (Callaghan Innovation), and Kerry Ryan (Principal Advisor for Secondary Transitions) – dived into how AI is changing the future of work and the skills and mindsets needed to adapt and thrive.


Guided by facilitator Steve Gadsby (Public Sector Lead APME, Consulting), the discussion highlighted why diversity in STEM is critical to building inclusive AI solutions that work for everyone. The event was proudly sponsored by HCLTech, and a visual “taste” of the evening – including photos and moments from the conversations – is now available via the online gallery and She Sharp’s Instagram for those who want to revisit the energy in the room.

The rise of Moltbook


A new security risk is emerging from “prompt worms” – self‑replicating AI instructions that can spread across interconnected agents and services.


OpenClaw, a vibe‑coded personal AI assistant that connects to WhatsApp, Slack and its own social network Moltbook, exemplifies this risk by linking many semi‑autonomous agents to private data and tools.​
Researchers have already uncovered hidden prompt‑injection posts, popular malicious “skills”, and a misconfigured database exposing API keys, emails and full write access to Moltbook posts.
Such weaknesses mean a single compromise could silently seed malicious prompts into feeds consumed by hundreds of thousands of agents, turning them into unwitting amplifiers.
As more powerful local models reduce reliance on centralised API “kill switches”, viral prompts – not sentient AI – may drive the next major, internet‑scale security incident.

AI and Talent in 2030: Four Futures for Work


This white paper outlines four plausible scenarios for how AI and talent trends could reshape jobs and the global economy by 2030, highlighting both systemic risks and new opportunities. It synthesises insights from chief strategy officers and experts to identify key uncertainties and “no‑regret” strategies that leaders can act on today. The report is designed to help organisations stress‑test their assumptions, build better foresight, and make more resilient strategic and investment decisions in an AI‑driven new economy. It forms part of the World Economic Forum’s Scenarios for the Global Economy Dialogue Series, which uses scenario analysis and cross‑industry dialogue to support decision‑makers navigating economic transformation.

The Great Land Grab: Billionaires Versus Communities


Five cutting-edge AI research concepts have been shortlisted to establish New Zealand’s first national AI research platform, backed by up to $70 million over seven years from the Institute for Advanced Technology. The selected proposals span bioeconomy-focused AI (BioAI), edge autonomy and autonomous systems (Aotearoa Institute for Autonomous Intelligence), large-scale agentic AI for productivity (Aotearoa Agentic AI Platform), creative and physical AI led by Wētā FX, and a joint Physical AI / New Zealand AI Platform for real-world systems. Each concept receives $250,000 in seed funding to develop detailed proposals, with a final platform decision expected in the first half of 2026 and full funding from July 2026. Collectively, they aim to accelerate commercialisation, grow high-tech jobs, strengthen industry–research partnerships, and embed Māori co-governance and data sovereignty. The initiative is positioned as a step-change in New Zealand’s AI capability and economic resilience, with government emphasising AI as a catalyst for sustainable growth and global competitiveness.

AI Forum Article




Management as AI superpower



This article by Ethan Mollick argues that in an era of powerful “agentic” AI, the key human advantage is not technical prompting but management skills: knowing what you want, delegating clearly, and judging quality. Ethan Mollick shows how executive MBA students used AI tools to build surprisingly advanced startup prototypes in just four days, largely because they could scope problems, define deliverables, and evaluate results effectively. He introduces a simple equation for deciding when to delegate to AI, balancing human baseline time, AI process time, and probability of success, and shows that with modern models this often saves substantial hours on complex tasks. The piece reframes prompts as “delegation documents” – akin to product requirements or project briefs – and suggests that traditional management capabilities (goal-setting, feedback, and review) are becoming an AI “superpower”. Ultimately, Mollick predicts that as everyone gains access to many cheap AI “agents”, those who thrive will be people who know what good looks like and can communicate it clearly enough that even an AI can deliver it.
