# The eighteen slide types

Every slide in a She Sharp deck is one of these. There is no free-form slide and
no "just put a text box here" — which is why the decks look like one thing
rather than eighteen things.

Pick the layout that matches the **shape** of what you are saying, not the
subject. Five things you want the room to weigh up are `themes`, whether they
are hackathon challenges, workshop tracks or panel topics.

---

## The whole set at a glance

| Type | What it looks like | Holds | Reach for it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `title` | Big headline on a dark slide, a few facts, a row of partner logos | 3 facts | Opening or closing the deck |
| `karakia` | Te reo on one side, English on the other, dark and quiet | Both languages, required | Opening and closing the event |
| `section` | A single chapter title, dark, optional photo behind | 1 title | Marking a new part of the day |
| `bullets` | Title and a short list | 5 bullets, 10 words each | A handful of separate points |
| `agenda` | Times down the left, short labels beside them | 14 rows | The run sheet |
| `people` | A grid of faces with names and job titles | Any number, density controls the size | Introducing the team, speakers, judges |
| `photo` | One photograph, full bleed, optional words over it | 1 image | Letting a moment land |
| `photo-grid` | Three to five photos as a composition | 3–5 images | Showing what an event feels like |
| `stats` | Three or four big numbers | 4 figures | Impact, scale, results |
| `logos` | Rows of logos on white chips, optionally grouped by tier | Any number, grouped | Sponsors and partners |
| `themes` | Cards, each a short title and one line | 6 cards | Options the room has to choose between |
| `criteria` | Named rows with a short description and a weight | 5 rows | How something is judged or assessed |
| `prizes` | Amounts with what they are for | 4 rows | The prize pool |
| `break` | Big countdown clock, dark | 1–120 minutes | Any break |
| `qr-cta` | One code, large, with up to three supporting lines | 1 code, 3 points | One thing you want the room to do now |
| `contact` | Social handles plus two or three codes | 3 codes | How to find She Sharp |
| `upcoming` | Two or three future events with dates | 3 events | The end, before the closing |
| `thanks` | Grouped logos plus names | Any number | Thanking partners and people |

Dark slides: `title`, `karakia`, `section`, `break`. Everything else is light,
matching the website. You can override it per slide, but the default is the
rhythm the deck was designed around — dark for the moments that want the room to
go quiet, light for everything informational.

---

## Which layout should I use for this?

| What you have | Use |
|---|---|
| A run sheet — times and what happens | `agenda` |
| Five challenge statements teams pick from | `themes` |
| A scoring rubric | `criteria` |
| "$5,000 for the winning team, $1,000 for runner-up" | `prizes` |
| Three speakers with headshots | `people` |
| Three things people need to know about the venue | `bullets` |
| A photo of last year's room | `photo` |
| Five photos of last year | `photo-grid` |
| "3000+ members, 94 events, 50 sponsors" | `stats` |
| Nine sponsor logos in two tiers | `logos` |
| "Scan this to give us feedback" | `qr-cta` |
| "Here's where to find us" with a couple of codes | `contact` |
| "We're back in September, and again in November" | `upcoming` |
| The end of a chapter, before the next one starts | `section` |
| Fifteen minutes for coffee | `break` |

Two rules of thumb worth applying before you pick anything:

- **Bullets are the default, and the default is usually wrong.** Almost every
  list of five items is really three themes, or four criteria, or a stats slide.
  The linter blocks a third bullet slide in a row for exactly this reason.
- **If two layouts both fit, pick the one with fewer words on screen.**

## Each type, in a bit more detail

### `title`

The event's name at full size on a dark slide, with up to three short facts
under it — a date, a venue, "free entry" — and a row of partner logos along the
bottom. Every deck opens with one; the closing "thank you for coming" version is
the same layout in its end variant.

> *Aotearoa AI Hackathon Festival 2026 · 7–8 August · AUT City Campus*

### `karakia`

A dark, quiet slide with the te reo on one side and the English translation on
the other. Both are required — the linter rejects one without the other, because
half a karakia on a screen is worse than none.

The opening (tīmatanga) and closing (whakamutunga) are fixed and come free with
every deck. You do not author these.

### `section`

One chapter title on a dark slide, optionally with a chapter number and a photo
behind it. It is a breath, not information. Use it when the day turns a corner —
after the welcome, before the workshop, before judging.

> *02 — The challenge*

### `bullets`

A title, optionally one sentence underneath, and up to five short lines. One or
two columns. Can carry a supporting image on the trailing edge, which is a good
way to stop a bullet slide feeling like a bullet slide.

Health & safety is the archetype: three things, six words each, and everyone in
the room needs all three.

### `agenda`

The run sheet. A clock time on the left, a very short label on the right, up to
fourteen rows. One row can be emphasised in the accent colour — use it for the
"you are here" moment, the thing the room actually came for.

A two-day event gets one slide per day rather than one slide with twenty-eight
rows.

> *5:45pm — Welcome and karakia*

### `people`

Faces in a grid with names and job titles. Three densities: large shows four
across with job titles, medium six across, small eight across with names only.
A missing headshot falls back to an initials tile rather than a gap.

Job titles are six words maximum. Nobody's biography goes here.

### `photo`

One photograph filling the slide, with an optional title and one line over it.
The overlay can be off, a flat scrim, or a gradient — use a scrim or gradient
whenever there is text, or the text disappears into the picture.

Reach for it when the thing you want is a feeling, not a fact: the room last
year, the winning team, the wall of sticky notes.

### `photo-grid`

Three to five photographs arranged as a composition. Fewer than three does not
read as a set; more than five stops reading as a composition and starts reading
as a contact sheet.

### `stats`

Three or four big numbers with a short label under each, and an optional
photograph beside them. Each number is written display-ready — `"3000+"`,
`"94"`, `"$10k"` — not calculated on screen.

Four is the maximum. A fifth number is not more impressive, it is less.

### `logos`

Sponsor and partner marks, optionally in labelled groups so tiers read clearly
("Principal partners", "Supporting partners"), and at three sizes. Logos sit on
white chips on dark slides, which is what keeps multi-colour marks looking
right — see `references/assets.md`.

### `themes`

Cards, each with a short title, one line of detail, and an optional tag. Up to
six. The detail is one line — the linter rejects two sentences in it.

This is the layout for things the room is going to choose between: hackathon
challenge areas, workshop tracks, panel topics, the three streams of a
conference.

### `criteria`

Named rows, each with a short description and an optional weight. Up to five.
Descriptions are ten words maximum, which is the whole point: a rubric on a
slide is a summary of a rubric, and the real one lives on the event page.

> *Impact — Solves a real problem for real people — 40%*

### `prizes`

Amounts with what each is for, up to four, optionally scoped to distinguish a
venue prize from a national one. A footnote line handles the "terms apply"
pointer without putting the terms on the slide.

### `break`

A dark slide with a countdown clock, sized to be read across a room. **Space
starts and pauses the clock** — on this slide only, Space does not advance.

Set the minutes to what the break actually is. The clock is the most useful
thing the deck does all day: a room comes back on time when it can see the time
running out.

### `qr-cta`

One large code with a label, a human-typable fallback line, and up to three
supporting points. This is the "do this now" slide — give feedback, sign up,
join Slack, submit your project.

One code per slide. Two codes side by side means the room scans neither.

### `contact`

She Sharp's social handles plus two or three codes. It sits near the front, so
someone who arrives curious can follow the organisation before the event has
even started.

Three codes is the hard maximum. A fourth makes all of them too small to scan.

### `upcoming`

Two or three future events, each with a title, a date and optionally a time,
venue, one-line blurb and image, plus a code linking to the events page.

These are **snapshotted when the deck is written**, not looked up live — a live
list would quietly change between the rehearsal and the day.

### `thanks`

The closing thank-you: grouped logos, plus people thanked by name — mentors,
judges, volunteers, the person who ran the door.

Put the names in. It is thirty seconds and it is the reason those people come
back.
