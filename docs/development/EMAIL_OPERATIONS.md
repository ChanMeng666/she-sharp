## Sending streams

**Sender identities live in `lib/email/senders.ts` — the single source of truth.** Never hard-code a From or Reply-To anywhere else. Four streams, and the stream decides everything downstream:

| Stream | Meaning | From | `List-Unsubscribe` | Honours opt-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `transactional` | Recipient-triggered, expected in minutes | `noreply@` (overridable by `EMAIL_FROM`) | No | **Never** |
| `notification` | Recurring, unrequested (reminders, announcements) | `noreply@` | Yes (RFC 8058) | Yes |
| `marketing` | Newsletter + one-off announcements | **`newsletter@`** | Yes (RFC 8058) — **ours** | Yes |
| `internal` | To She Sharp's own mailboxes | `noreply@` | No | No |

- `sendEmail()` (`lib/email/service.ts`) takes `stream`, defaulting to `transactional`. It resolves From/Reply-To, attaches one-click unsubscribe headers for `notification` **and `marketing`**, tags every send `stream:<name>` for per-stream Resend analytics, and checks `email_optouts` for those same two streams — a suppressed address must still receive a password reset, so `transactional` and `internal` are exempt from both.
- **`marketing` was added to both of those on purpose, and its absence was a real hole.** While broadcasts owned marketing mail, Resend attached the unsubscribe header and honoured its own opt-outs, so neither gap showed. Self-hosted, the same code would have sent a newsletter with no one-click opt-out, and would have ignored a one-click opt-out already recorded here — an unsubscribe that visibly does nothing is how a list earns spam complaints, and the complaint ceiling is account-wide. The reasoning is written at both sites: `isSuppressed()` in `lib/email/optouts.ts` and `buildStreamHeaders()` in `service.ts`.
- **There are two senders, not one**, which is why the header assembly lives in `lib/email/unsubscribe-headers.ts` rather than inside `service.ts`. `sendEmail()` sends one message at a time and imports the database; the newsletter and event batches are built offline by `scripts/` and handed to the Resend CLI without ever passing through `sendEmail()`. Both paths must attach the headers, and a template that cannot know the address at authoring time emits `UNSUBSCRIBE_URL_PLACEHOLDER` (`%%SHESHARP_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL%%`) for `substituteUnsubscribeUrl()` to swap per recipient — in the text part as well as the HTML. It is deliberately not brace-delimited, because `gateMergeTags` fails any unknown `{{…}}`. It replaced Resend's `{{{RESEND_UNSUBSCRIBE_URL}}}`, which only ever worked for broadcasts and would have shipped as literal text through the batch endpoint.
- `EMAIL_FROM` overrides the **transactional** From only. Letting it reach marketing is what previously sent the monthly newsletter from `noreply@` while its own footer said "just hit reply".
- **`newsletter@` is a continuity decision, not a preference.** The live newsletter still goes out via **Mailchimp** from `She Sharp <newsletter@shesharp.org.nz>` (DKIM `k2`/`k3._domainkey` → `dkim*.mcsv.net`). The founder wants to replace Mailchimp with Resend *to improve deliverability*, so the visible sender must not change across that move — see the Mailchimp → Resend section in the deployment doc, especially the list-hygiene warning (Mailchimp's years of bounce/unsubscribe suppression are **not** in the subscriber CSV export) and the rule to do the ESP migration and the DMARC tightening in **separate months**.
- `info@` is the approved sender for **1:1** mail only (contact replies, event fulfilment). List mail uses `newsletter@`. It replaced `hello@` on 2026-08-23, when a delivery probe found `hello@` had never been created — note that sending *as* a non-existent address works fine, because Resend signs at the domain; what breaks is the recipient pressing Reply.
- **`newsletter@` is never a Reply-To.** It accepts mail, but nobody on the team had its password as of 2026-08-17 and a direct question in Slack about whether anyone read it went unanswered. The `marketing` Reply-To is `info@`; the From stays `newsletter@` for the reason above.
- **One-click unsubscribe**: `lib/email/unsubscribe-token.ts` (stateless HMAC over the email *hash*, never the address) → `app/api/email/unsubscribe/route.ts`. POST is unauthenticated and must return a bare 200 (providers treat 3xx as failure); **GET must never mutate** — link scanners prefetch it.
- **Bounces/complaints**: `app/api/webhooks/resend/route.ts` verifies Svix signatures by hand (`lib/email/webhook-verify.ts`, no `svix` dependency) and writes `email_optouts`; complaints also post to Slack. Needs `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.
- **Two suppression stores, one join key**: `email_optouts` (runtime) and `lib/data/json/email-suppression-hashes.json` (committed, read by the offline scripts) both key on `hashEmail()` from `lib/email/hash.ts`. Reconcile monthly with `npx tsx scripts/email/suppression.ts sync`. Since the subscriber table arrived there is a **third** store to keep honest, and `npx tsx scripts/email/suppression.ts reconcile` reports the drift — subscribers who also sit on a suppression register, and which act came last. It is a report; it changes nothing.
- **The committed register is no longer a frozen snapshot.** It was the 2026-08-17 Mailchimp export and nothing else; `npx tsx scripts/email/suppression.ts pull-mailchimp [--since <ISO>] [--full] [--dry-run]` now folds in unsubscribes and cleaned addresses straight from the Mailchimp API, incrementally. **Run it before any import.** The first run, on 2026-08-27, took it from 2,129 to **2,138** — three unsubscribes and six hard bounces that had happened in the ten days since the export, and that an import taken from that export would have emailed. The run immediately before the 2026-08-29 import took it to **2,144**: six *more* people had left in the two days since. Six of the fifteen rows that import held back were those six. The habit is the point, not the numbers — every gap between an export and an import fills with people who left.
- **Checks**: `npx tsx lib/email/hardening.test.ts`.
- **One domain on purpose**: transactional and marketing share `shesharp.org.nz`. The pre-committed trigger to split marketing onto `news.` is complaint rate >0.10%, a single send >1,000 recipients, or hard bounces >2% — see the deployment doc.

### The one blocker on DMARC

Enabling Google DKIM needs **Google Workspace super-admin**, and `website@shesharp.org.nz` (the maintainer's account) **cannot open `admin.google.com`**. Without it, **do not go past `p=quarantine`** — at `p=reject` a forwarded message from a team mailbox is destroyed rather than filed in Junk, with no bounce and no way to find out. Stopping permanently at quarantine is a defensible end state; `docs/deployment/EMAIL_AUTHENTICATION.md` has the exact request to send an admin.

What does **not** need a mailbox login: sending *as* `info@`/`newsletter@` (Resend signs at the domain level), Reply-To to team inboxes (the point is that replies reach the team, not the maintainer), and DMARC report collection (Cloudflare receives on its own domain).

A monthly email newsletter built on **React Email**, rendered in this repo and sent through **Resend's transactional batch API** — not broadcasts. Each issue is a JSON file at `lib/data/json/newsletter-issues/<YYYY-MM>.json` split into two blocks (`lib/newsletter/schema.ts`): a machine-owned **`auto`** snapshot (events + stats, refreshed freely) and a human-owned **`editorial`** block (founder note, spotlight, photo of the month, subject/preview, CTA) that regeneration must never overwrite.

**Why batch and not broadcasts.** A broadcast held its own contact list and attached its own unsubscribe link, which meant Resend was the consent record by construction. The batch endpoint does neither, so both had to be built here — and that is the point: the record of who consented is a table we own, back-uppable, queryable and portable to any ESP. The cost is that every guarantee the broadcast used to supply is now ours to enforce, which is why `build-newsletter-batch.ts` refuses to write anything without `EMAIL_UNSUBSCRIBE_SECRET`.

### Who is on the list

`newsletter_subscribers` (`lib/db/schema/system.ts`), status `pending | subscribed | unsubscribed | bounced | complained`. **Only `subscribed` is mailable.** The row records who, when (`consentDate`, `confirmedAt`), by which route (`source`, `consentSource`), the IP and user agent when the website form captured them, the current state, and when they left.

**1,545 rows, and all of them arrived the same way.** The 2026-08-29 import carried the Mailchimp audience across: 1,560 rows read from the 2026-08-17 `subscribed` export, **15** held back by the suppression register, 0 malformed. They all carry `source = 'mailchimp-import'`, a `consentSource` sentence naming the audience and the export, and a real `confirmedAt` from the export's `CONFIRM_TIME` — every one of the 1,560 had one, which is why the import could record a double opt-in without a confirmation click of ours. `consentIp` and `consentUserAgent` are null for all of them: the export's sign-up IPs were deliberately not imported. So `source` is currently the only thing distinguishing an imported row from a website sign-up, and it is the column to filter on when you need that distinction. Importer: `scripts/email/import-mailchimp-subscribers.ts` — dry-run by default, refuses the `unsubscribed`/`cleaned`/`nonsubscribed` exports by filename, prints counts and truncated hashes and never an address.

**Double opt-in, and the second step is a POST.** `POST /api/newsletter/subscribe` writes a `pending` row and emails a link; `/newsletter/confirm` renders a **button**, and only the button press confirms. Confirmation must never happen on a GET — corporate mail gateways, link scanners and inbox previewers fetch every URL in a message, and a GET-confirm subscribes people who never clicked. The public endpoint answers `{ ok: true }` for any well-formed input whatever actually happened, so it cannot be used to test whether an address is on the list; `scripts/email/inspect-subscribers.ts` is how a human checks a row, masked.

Logic is in `lib/newsletter/subscribers.ts` — `subscribe()`, `confirmSubscription()`, `unsubscribeByHash()`, `markBouncedByHash()`, `markComplainedByHash()`, `listSubscribed()`, `sweepExpiredTokens()`. The one-click unsubscribe endpoint and the Resend webhook both call into it, so an opt-out, a bounce and a complaint each move the subscriber row as well as writing `email_optouts`.

**Run the monthly loop with the `/monthly-newsletter` skill** (`.claude/skills/monthly-newsletter/SKILL.md`): fetch the staged draft → register the issue in `lib/newsletter/issues-registry.ts` (one import + one map line per month) → curate photos → edit `editorial` → preview (`scripts/newsletter/preview.ts`) → test-send (`scripts/newsletter/send-test.ts`) → commit + deploy → approve (`scripts/newsletter/approve.ts`) → build the recipients file (`scripts/email/recipients-from-db.ts --key newsletter-<issue>`) → build the batch files (`scripts/newsletter/build-newsletter-batch.ts <issue> --recipients …`) → **a human runs the printed `resend emails batch` commands.**

**Approving is not sending.** `scripts/newsletter/approve.ts` and the endpoint behind it mark the issue cleared and record the intended send slot; they contact Resend not at all. The send is a CLI step on purpose: it joins the pipeline the four outbound skills already share, it keeps a human in the loop by construction, and a fan-out of one request per 100 recipients has no business inside a serverless function bounded by `maxDuration` and by Neon's connection-burst limit. Both the endpoint response and `approve.ts` print the two next commands, because the step that used to happen automatically now happens on somebody's laptop.

**`build-newsletter-batch.ts` enforces four things before it writes a byte**: `assertSendAllowed` (a newsletter aimed at event registrants stops here, not in an inbox); a signed per-recipient `List-Unsubscribe` pair plus a substituted opt-out URL in body and text; the strict broadcast gates run **once** over the rendered issue, because a clipped 120KB issue or a WebP hero is a whole-list problem rather than a per-message one; and a hash ledger, so a resumed or repeated run cannot double-mail.

## Outbound email skills

Four guided skills let non-technical teammates send email without writing code. All four share one pipeline — **repo scripts render, the Resend CLI sends** — so `lib/email/service.ts` (the single `resend.emails.send()` behind every template call site, module-level Resend singleton) stays untouched while `--reply-to`, `--cc`, `--tags`, `--scheduled-at`, batch and broadcasts all come from the CLI.

| Skill | Use it for | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| `/reply-to-contact-messages` | Answering the contact form backlog (DB authoritative, Slack `C0AGVRL0G5A` cross-checked, `reviewed_at` = handled) | Tier 1 — 1:1 only |
| `/update-mailing-list` | Roster reporting + consent-gated CSV import + hashed suppression list | manages Tier 0 |
| `/send-event-emails` | Four stage emails to one event's Humanitix registrants, chunked and resumable | Tier 2 — fulfilment only |
| `/email-the-community` | One-off gated announcement to the whole subscriber list | Tier 0 only |

- **Shared layer**: `lib/email/message.ts` (`MessageSpec` — 9 content blocks, no HTML for the author), `compose.tsx` (dual engine: `layout` reuses `lib/email/layout.ts` for transactional, `react` renders `emails/announcement.tsx` for broadcasts; also exports `withDraftBanner`), `gates.ts` (100KB / absolute-URL / JPEG-only / unsubscribe / merge-tag / secret-scan / **from-identity / reply-to-domain / tag-charset / no-reply-path**, plus an advisory `Redactions to confirm` list), `audience.ts` (`assertSendAllowed` — marketing to Tier ≥1 throws).
- **Shared CLI**: `scripts/email/` — `render-message.ts` (spec → `tmp/emails/*.html` + gates + a paste-able `resend` command), `normalize-recipients.ts` (any-shape CSV: detects headers, reads the guess back in plain English, never asks anyone to edit a CSV), `recipients-from-db.ts` (the same recipients-file shape, built from the subscriber table — the bridge that replaced Resend segments), `mailable.ts` (`selectMailable()`: the one implementation of "who may actually receive this", shared by the builder and by `suppression.ts reconcile`), `build-batch.ts` (per-person render, 100/chunk, idempotency keys), `audience-report.ts`, `inspect-subscribers.ts` (masked read of a subscriber row, for verifying the opt-in flow by hand), `mark-contact-replied.ts`, `suppression.ts`.
- **Re-subscription is the subtle rule in `mailable.ts`.** A suppressed address is not permanently excluded: `consent-rules.md` says the one way back is the person themselves through the website form, so a confirmation *later* than the suppression wins and they are mailable again. Without that, someone who unsubscribed in 2024 and signed up this morning would confirm, see themselves on the list, and never receive anything — invisible from outside. **A spam complaint is the exception and is never reversed**, whatever the timestamps say.
- **Consent is the load-bearing rule**: the subscription record is the `newsletter_subscribers` table, and only a `subscribed` row — reached by double opt-in — is mailable. Resend segments and topics are no longer consulted for it. Registering, donating, applying or writing in is **not** subscribing — see `.claude/skills/update-mailing-list/references/consent-rules.md`, which the other three skills defer to.
- **Two-stage confirmation everywhere**: render + gate locally → `resend … --dry-run` prints the full request JSON without calling the API → only then the real send. `resend emails batch` has **no** `--dry-run`; its equivalent preflight is the local render plus `--batch-validation strict`.
- **Zero migrations**: `form_status` has no `replied` value, so contact replies are recorded with the existing `reviewed_at` / `status` / `review_notes` columns.

## Email addresses and routing

`industry@` for sponsorship, `mentoring@` for the mentorship programme,
`events@` for a specific event, `people@` for applications, `info@` for
everything else. Held in `lib/config/contact-addresses.ts`.

**Publish nothing that is not in that file.** On 2026-08-23 a delivery probe
found that seven of the eleven addresses this site published had never been
created and hard-bounced everything sent to them; they had been invented by
page templates in 2025. The evidence, the replacements and the rerun command
are in `docs/development/EMAIL_ADDRESSES.md`, and the part only the Workspace
super-admin can fix is in `docs/deployment/WORKSPACE_MAILBOX_CHECKLIST.md`.
